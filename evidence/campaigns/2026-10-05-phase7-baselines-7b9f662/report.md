# Campaign: 2026-10-05-phase7-baselines-7b9f662 — generic-compressor baseline ladder

Phase 7.0/7.0b measured VOLE's candidates against `BYTE_RANS`, a whole-file
**order-0 byte-rANS** lane with **no LZ / no long-range matching**. That is not a
generic-compressor baseline, so a "win over `BYTE_RANS`" says nothing about how
VOLE compares to the compressors a practitioner would actually use. This receipt
adds the missing ladder and states VOLE's honest standing.

It is a new, separately scoped measurement. **No wire format, candidate, or
decode path changed** (`binary_sha256 = fd14c0c6…`, identical to the Phase-7.0b
producers campaign). The `producers` corpus files were generated by the pinned
Phase-7.0b generator; the `phase7` corpus files by `tools/pdf-corpus.sh`.

## Method

For every file in both corpora, `tools/baselines.sh` records:

- **source** size;
- the smallest **lossless complete-file** size for `gzip -9`, `zstd -19
  --long=27`, `xz -9e` and `brotli -q 11` — each result is decompressed and
  `cmp`'d against the source *before* it is scored (a failure would be `null`);
- every **VOLE lane** (complete serialized `.voldoc` size): the unforced auto
  winner, plus forced `raw`, `rle`, `byte-rans`, `pdf-physical`, `pdf-channels`,
  `pdf-layout`, `pdf-layout-rans`, `pdf-deflate-replay`, `pdf-deflate-replay-rans`.
  A forced kind the input does not propose is a typed `Usage` decline (`null`).

`best_vole` is the **minimum across all VOLE lanes**, i.e. the most favourable
VOLE number. Every auto winner is `verify`'d and `decode`d + `cmp`'d byte-exact
(27/27). Tools: `gzip 1.12`, `zstd 1.5.4`, `xz 5.4.1`, `brotli 1.0.9`, `jq 1.6`,
in the opt-in `baseline` image
(`debian`→`dev` pinned base `rust:1.99.0-slim-bookworm@sha256:452176c0…`).

**On 27 corpus files, the best VOLE lane beats `gzip -9`, `zstd -19`, `xz -9e`
and `brotli -q 11` on 0 files.** The best generic compressor is smaller than the
best VOLE lane on **every** file; summed across the corpus, VOLE is
**460,320 bytes larger** than the best generic compressor
(`phase7` 410,355 B over 23 files; `producers` 49,965 B over 4 files).

## Complete per-file table

The full 27-row table is `baseline-table.md` in this receipt. Every row carries
the source size, all four generic sizes, `BYTE_RANS`, the best VOLE lane and its
name, and `VOLE − best generic` (positive = VOLE loses). A row's `rank` field in
`results.json` gives the ascending order of `raw`, `gzip`, `zstd`, `xz`,
`brotli`, `BYTE_RANS` and `best_vole` for that file. The generic compressors are
1–2 orders of magnitude ahead on the low-entropy fixtures (e.g.
`hand-base2.pdf`: `xz -9e` 3,524 B vs best VOLE 56,885 B; `_synthetic/bigtext.pdf`:
`xz -9e` 824 B vs 38,215 B).

## Conclusion — the decisive methodology result

Prior "wins" were relative to a **weak order-0** baseline (`BYTE_RANS`), not to
generic compression. Under the honest ladder — where the complete file must
losslessly recover the original, so all framing/model overhead is charged — **VOLE
loses to every generic compressor on every file tested, including the two files
the Phase-6/7.0 sharing narrative rested on.** This corroborates the Phase-7.0b
finding that the Cairo delta is a repeated-identical-bytes region that LZ
captures far better. VOLE's exact structural reconstruction remains byte-exact
and is unchanged; its **compression** claim does not survive a generic baseline
on this corpus. No candidate is adopted, removed, or changed.
