# Phase 22.1 — SQLite competitive envelope (equal-contract court)

The historical control (`tools/fixtures/phase18-contract-packed.py`, with `journal_mode=WAL`, `synchronous=NORMAL`, and TWO full FTS5 indexes) is retained as a HISTORICAL CONTROL lane (`hist`). The envelope adds six purpose-tuned configurations. Same 12-document subset, same contract depths C0–C5, same answer SQL (the frozen control's `sql_for`), same accounting (persistent bytes = **sum of regular-file sizes**; `du -sb` is never used). Wall times are integer microseconds; `N=10` repetitions, paired and interleaved.

## Durability contracts (compared explicitly, not assumed equal)

**SQLite `journal_mode=WAL`, `synchronous=NORMAL`** (the matched setting for the headline, matching VOLE's unproven power-loss boundary): the WAL keeps the database *internally consistent* after a crash, but SQLite documents that under certain power failures the **latest committed transactions may be lost**. There is no torn page and no corruption; the committed prefix is recoverable. `synchronous=FULL` would `fsync` the WAL on every commit (removing that exposure) at a per-commit latency cost; the `--durability full` variant of this fixture measures that cost and is reported separately in `raw/durability_full.*`.

**VOLE packed store (ADR-0053; Phase 20.1)** with `SyncPolicy::Batch` (the default): records are forced to stable storage once per segment (seal/flush), the field manifest is **flushed before it is published**, and recovery is a strict **prefix** — no partial node is ever fetchable and every node is re-hashed against its id. A crash can therefore lose records appended since the last flush, but a published manifest is always consistent. Phase 20.1's 1,300-case fault-injection court passed under both policies with **zero** bad-hash and **zero** prefix violations, but it injects process death (`SIGKILL`/`abort`), which leaves the OS page cache intact: **no `fsync` boundary is actually exercised**, so true power loss and torn/lost rename remain **argued, not measured**.

**Net.** Both sides at the matched setting can lose the tail of the most recent writes after a power failure; both preserve consistency and never expose a partial record. Neither side has a measured true-power-loss receipt. SQLite `synchronous=FULL` is the stronger, slower option; VOLE's `--sync=each` (per-record `fdatasync`) is its analogue and was also not measured under true power loss.

## Caveats and honesty

- The store is the bind-mounted `/work`; per-cell CV is in `raw/per_doc_lane_depth.csv`. Wide intervals are reported wide.
- `full`/`hybrid` exclude FTS because the contract has **no search observation**; `hist` carries the control's two FTS indexes and `fts` carries one tuned index, so the FTS overhead is quantified, not hidden.
- `hybrid`'s 'native parser/cache' is Poppler (PDF) + Python stdlib (OOXML/EPUB) — the strongest native extraction available in the pinned image without modifying `src/`; it is NOT a rewrite of VOLE's adapters.
- Cells are pooled medians; the paired per-rep CI is the estimator for ratio claims (ADR-0054). `hist` is measured only at C0 and C5 as a control point, not at every depth.
